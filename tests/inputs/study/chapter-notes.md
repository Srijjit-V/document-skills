# Chapter 7 notes: the water cycle and watersheds (Earth Science, Ms. Reyes)

Exam is Friday November 6, 40 questions. Ms. Reyes said: know the terms, the order of the cycle, what a watershed is, and the freshwater numbers.

## Where Earth's water is
- About 97% of Earth's water is salt water (oceans).
- About 2.5% is fresh water.
- Of the fresh water: about 69% is frozen in glaciers and ice caps, about 30% is groundwater, and less than 1% is surface water (lakes, rivers, swamps).
- Only a tiny share of fresh water is easy for people to use.

## The cycle, in order
1. Evaporation: liquid water becomes vapor, driven by heat from the sun. Happens at any temperature, not only at boiling.
2. Transpiration: plants release water vapor through their leaves. Different from evaporation because it comes from living plants.
3. Condensation: water vapor cools and becomes liquid droplets. This forms clouds.
4. Precipitation: water falls as rain, snow, sleet, or hail.
5. Runoff: water that flows over land into streams and rivers.
6. Infiltration: water soaking into the soil. It can recharge groundwater.
Water can follow many paths, so the cycle is not one fixed line.

## Residence time
Residence time is how long a water molecule stays in one place.
- Atmosphere: about 9 days.
- Rivers: about 2 weeks.
- Ocean: about 3,200 years.
- Glaciers and ice caps: can be thousands of years.

## Groundwater words
- Aquifer: layer of rock or sediment that holds and moves groundwater.
- Water table: the top of the zone where the ground is completely saturated. It rises and falls and is NOT flat; it follows the land a little.
- Recharge: water added to an aquifer by infiltration.

## Watersheds
A watershed is the land area that drains to one outlet, such as a river, lake, or the sea. Its boundary is a ridge or high ground called a divide. Everything that happens in a watershed (fertilizer, pollution, paving) can affect the water at the outlet.
Paved surfaces cut infiltration and increase runoff, so streams can rise faster after storms.
Simple water budget from class: Runoff = Precipitation minus Infiltration minus Evaporation (this ignores plants and storage).

## Common mistakes Ms. Reyes warned about
- Mixing up transpiration (plants) and evaporation (surfaces).
- Saying the water table is flat.
- Thinking the water cycle starts at the ocean. It has no starting point.
- Forgetting that fresh water is only about 2.5%, not "most" of Earth's water.
