These three sources are fictional, written for this test.

Source A
Rivera, M. (2021). Later start times and attendance. Journal of Fictional Education, 12(3), 44-58.
Summary: Followed 4,200 students in one district. After the first bell moved 45 minutes later, attendance rose from 91.4% to 93.5%, a gain of 2.1 percentage points. The gain was larger for grades 11 and 12 (3.0 points).

Source B
Chen, L., & Okoye, A. (2022). Start times and test scores in one district. Journal of Fictional Education, 14(1), 12-30.
Summary: Studied 3,900 students. In the first year after a later start, test scores did not change significantly: the mean difference was 0.4 points on a 100-point scale (p = .62). The authors say longer follow-up is needed.

Source C
Brooks, D. (2023). Bus schedules under later bell times. Fictional Transportation Review, 9(2), 101-117.
Summary: Sampled 15 districts that moved to later starts. Eleven of the 15 needed an extra bus tier. Transportation costs rose an average of 6.2% of the transportation budget.
